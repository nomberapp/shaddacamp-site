The experience

Live inside a working camel camp, not a tourist camp built for photos.

What is a shadda?

In the local dialect, a shadda is the move a camel camp makes when the grazing runs out: everything is packed up, loaded and carried to new ground.

Camel men have moved this way for generations, following the rain and the grass. A Shadda journey lets you join them for a few days, take part in the work, and see the desert the way they live it.

What you'll do

Move with the herd

Pack the camp, load the trucks, and walk or ride with the camels to the next grazing ground.

Arabic coffee and hosting

Learn to roast, brew and serve coffee the way it's offered to honoured guests.

Cook over fire

Prepare traditional desert dishes with the camel men and eat together on the ground, sharing one tray.

Ride with the herd

Ride a camel for a stretch of each move, with a handler beside you, or walk with the herd on foot.

Nights under the stars

Sleep far from city lights, with stories by the fire before bed.

A Bedouin night game

Hunt in the dark for a small white object, shout "Legita!" when you find it, and race for the circle before you're caught.

A day at camp

  1. Dawn

    Wake with the camp

    Prayer time for the camel men, then the herd is fed and milked while the first coffee is brewed.

  2. Morning

    Work and learn

    Help with the camels, gather firewood, fix what needs fixing, and learn a new skill each day.

  3. Midday

    Rest in the shade

    Lunch together, then a long rest through the hottest hours.

  4. Afternoon

    Out with the herd

    Follow the camels to graze, explore the area, or practise what you've learned.

  5. Evening

    Fire, food and stories

    Dinner around the fire, coffee for everyone, and a night sky full of stars.

See the week, day by day

One week, one shadda

Six days with a working camel camp, from Saturday to Thursday. Here's how the week unfolds.

  1. FridayHotel package

    Arrive in Riyadh

    A comfortable bed and a good night's sleep before the desert.

    Check in to your 5-star hotel in Riyadh and rest. Camp-only guests join us there on Saturday morning.

  2. SaturdayInto the desert

    The road out

    The city falls away behind you, and the sand takes over.

    • 9:00 AMLeave the hotel together for the four-hour drive west to the desert around Al Dawadmi.
    • LunchArrive at camp. Your room is ready, and the coffee is on.
    • EveningWelcome dinner of kabsa, with dates and qahwa by the fire.
  3. SundaySettle in

    Meet the herd

    A slow day to find your feet before the move.

    Walk out with the camels as they graze, taste fresh camel milk, and take your first short camel ride with a handler. In the late afternoon, try sandboarding on the nearest dunes. By sunset you'll know how the camp works, and what tomorrow asks of everyone.

  4. MondayThe first shadda

    The whole camp moves

    Torches in the dark, camels rising, and a camp folded away before sunrise.

    • 5:00 AMThe camp wakes to prepare for the move.
    • 6:00 AMThe herd sets off. Workers pack up the camp and hitch the trailer rooms, and you can lend a hand if you like.
    • MorningChoose your way: ride a camel or walk with the herd for a stretch, drive alongside it, or go with the caravan 25 km ahead to set up coffee and breakfast and start on lunch.
    • LunchThe camels catch up and everyone eats together. At 3:00 PM the camels drink and move on, and the caravan heads for the new camp.
    • 4:30 PMThe caravan reaches the new camp spot and the rooms are set up.
    • 5:30 PMThe herd walks in for its evening feed.
    • 7:00 PMDinner at a camp that didn't exist this morning, then stargazing far from any city light.
  5. TuesdayThe second shadda

    On the move again

    This time you know the rhythm, and the desert ahead is new.

    Another dawn start and another day following the herd to fresh grazing. Swap if you like: if you went with the caravan on Monday, ride or walk with the camels today. Lunch is bread baked in the ashes of the fire. In the evening, the camel men share stories and poetry around the fire.

  6. WednesdayFull day at camp

    Qahwa and a night game

    No move today. Just the camp, the coffee and the people.

    • MorningLearn to roast, brew and pour qahwa the way the camel men do, and how to serve it to a guest.
    • After darkAn hour of Sarao Allak Barge. A small white object is thrown into the dark. Whoever finds it shouts "Legita!" and runs for a circle drawn in the sand, while everyone else tries to catch him first.
  7. ThursdayFarewell

    Back to the city

    One last coffee, and goodbyes that take longer than planned.

    • 9:00 AMLeave camp after breakfast.
    • NoonArrive back at the hotel in Riyadh. Hotel package guests stay the night and check out on Friday.

What you'll eat

Food cooked over fire by the camp, the way the camel men eat.

  • Mufattah, a whole lamb roasted and served on rice
  • Kabsa, rice and slow-cooked meat, served on a shared tray
  • Jareesh, cracked wheat cooked with spices
  • Bread baked in the ashes of the fire
  • Fresh camel milk, dates and qahwa through the day
  • Vegetarian meals on request

Camp etiquette

You're a guest in someone's camp. A few customs go a long way.

  1. Coffee

    Accept with your right hand

    Coffee is poured in small amounts and refilled. When you've had enough, gently shake the cup from side to side before handing it back.

  2. Meals

    Eat from your side of the tray

    Meals are often shared from one large tray. Use your right hand and eat from the part nearest you.

  3. Prayer

    Give space at prayer times

    The camel men pray five times a day. Keep noise down and avoid walking in front of anyone praying.

  4. Photos

    Always ask first

    Ask before photographing any person. Some camel men are happy to be filmed; some are not.

  5. Dress

    Dress modestly

    Loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees, for everyone.

  6. Animals

    Follow the herders

    Camels are large and strong. Approach only when a herder says it's safe.

Questions

The things guests ask us most.

Do I need a visa?

Most visitors do. Many nationalities can get a tourist eVisa online. See Travel help for the official links.

Where does the trip start and where is the camp?

It starts and ends at a 5-star hotel in Riyadh. The camp is in the desert around Al Dawadmi, about four hours' drive west. We send the exact meeting point after booking.

Can I stay just a night or two?

Yes. In the weeks between Shadda departures you can book a camp stay at SAR 4,775 per bed per night, with three meals a day, snacks and qahwa. You drive to the camp yourself, and we send the location once your beds are confirmed. There is no deposit: pay any time before check-in. Camp stays run all year except Ramadan, July and August.

How much does it cost?

SAR 22,375 per person for the camp week, Saturday to Thursday, or SAR 24,375 with a hotel night in the city before and after.

How often are departures?

Every other week, so two departures a month through the season. Each departure is confirmed once at least 15 guests have booked.

When are the departures?

Every other Saturday from 16 January to 24 April 2027, with no departures during Ramadan. The full list is on the Packages page. Summer is too hot for guests in the open desert.

Are there showers and toilets?

Yes. Every trailer room has its own bathroom with a toilet and hot shower.

Will I ride a camel?

If you want to. You can ride a stretch of each move with a handler, walk with the herd, or travel by car.

I'm travelling alone. Can I join?

Yes. You can share a twin room with another solo guest of the same gender, or have a single room for an extra SAR 1,500. There are five single rooms on each departure, given in booking order. On the hotel package it's SAR 2,500, which includes a single hotel room.

Do I need to be fit?

You should be comfortable walking a few kilometres on sand and sitting on the ground at meals and around the fire. Tell us about any health needs when you book and we'll advise.

What should I wear?

Loose, modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees, a warm layer for the nights, a scarf or hat for the sun, and closed shoes. See the packing list on the Prepare page.

What about food and water?

All meals, coffee and drinking water are included. Vegetarian meals are available on request.

Is it safe?

Every trip has a trained guide, first aid on site, a support vehicle and a way to call for help at all times.

Can I use my phone?

Coverage comes and goes in the desert. We encourage guests to switch off and be present, and we always keep a working line for emergencies.

What ID do I need to book?

A Saudi national ID for citizens, an Iqama for residents, or a passport for visitors, for every guest.

Can I book for someone else?

Yes. Choose "Someone else, not me" on the booking form and enter each guest's details.

How do payment and cancellation work?

A 30% deposit holds your place, and the balance is due 30 days before departure. Cancel 30 or more days ahead for a full refund minus the SAR 500 booking fee per guest. See the cancellation policy for the rest.
